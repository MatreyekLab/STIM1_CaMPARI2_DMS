# STIM1_CaMPARI2_DMS
Data and scripts for STIM1 DMS with CaMPARI2 manuscript
Abstract:
Variants of Uncertain Significance (VUS) account for nearly half of the ~3.5 million protein variants seen in sequenced human genomes, hindering the application of genomic data for medicine. Assigning pathogenicity or benignity to many of these variants will require multiplexed genetic assays capturing cellular phenotypes that correlate to disease severity. This is particularly important for proteins that are difficult to model, such as STIM1, an endoplasmic reticulum (ER) integral protein that senses ER-luminal calcium to conformationally rearrange, oligomerize, and trigger cytoplasmic calcium influx. Pathogenic STIM1 variants cause a range of clinical presentations  including muscle weakness, cognitive dysfunction, constitutive platelet activation, or combined immunodeficiency. Many pathogenic variants are in the 36 residue EF-hand domain that senses the depletion of ER calcium and triggers SOCE, alongside 20 VUS that need to be characterized. We evaluated a set of functional assays to identify readouts that best discriminated pathogenic from benign STIM1 variants. We applied the assays to a panel of 7 pathogenic STIM1 variants and 11 VUS. We found that cytoplasmic calcium as measured by CaMPARI2 biosensor photoconversion, cell survival, and ER puncta formation were the three most informative phenotypic assay readouts. We obtained CAMPARI2 photoconversion scores for 746 variants of the STIM1 EF-hand domain. These scores accurately distinguished disease-associated variants in patients from variants only found in healthy exomic databases, outperforming computational predictors. Using these variants as benchmarks, we classified 11 STIM1 VUS as predicted benign, and 8 VUS as predicted pathogenic. We prospectively classified 106 products of single nucleotide variants as predicted benign, and 98 as predicted pathogenic. With its multiplexability, CaMPARI2 photoconversion enables a new route for experimentally characterizing and classifying variants in genes encoding proteins that impact intracellular calcium signaling.
